# topic: Generative Models
# id: genmodels
# subtopics: Autoencoders and VAEs (ELBO, reparameterization); GANs and their failure modes; Diffusion models (DDPM, DDIM, noise schedules); Classifier-free guidance; Latent diffusion; Flow matching and rectified flow; Normalizing flows and autoregressive models; Evaluation (FID, likelihood)

Q: The VAE training objective, the ELBO, consists of [E]
- an adversarial loss and a gradient penalty
* a reconstruction term and a KL divergence between the approximate posterior and the prior
- a contrastive loss and a weight decay term
- a likelihood term only
> ELBO = E_q[log p(x|z)] - KL(q(z|x) || p(z)), a lower bound on log p(x).

Q: The reparameterization trick writes z = mu + sigma * eps with eps ~ N(0, I) so that [M]
- sampling becomes deterministic at test time
* gradients can flow through the sampling step to the encoder parameters
- the KL term becomes zero
- the decoder is not needed
> Moving the randomness into a parameter-free noise variable makes z a differentiable function of mu and sigma.

Q: "Posterior collapse" in VAEs refers to [H]
- the decoder output diverging
* the approximate posterior matching the prior so the decoder ignores the latent variable
- the encoder producing infinite variance
- the reconstruction loss reaching zero
> With a powerful (for example autoregressive) decoder, the KL term can be minimized by making z uninformative. KL annealing and free bits are common remedies.

Q: In a GAN, the generator is trained to [E]
- maximize the likelihood of the data
* produce samples that the discriminator classifies as real
- reconstruct its input
- minimize the discriminator's weights
> The two networks play a minimax game; at the optimum of the original formulation the generator minimizes the Jensen-Shannon divergence to the data distribution.

Q: Mode collapse in GANs means [M]
- the discriminator's loss reaches zero
* the generator produces only a small subset of the modes of the data distribution
- training runs out of memory
- the latent space becomes discrete
> The generator can fool the discriminator with a few convincing outputs; remedies include minibatch discrimination, unrolled GANs and Wasserstein losses.

Q: The Wasserstein GAN replaces the original loss mainly to [H]
- speed up sampling
* provide useful gradients even when real and generated distributions have little overlap
- remove the need for a critic
- generate discrete data
> The earth-mover distance varies smoothly with the generator's parameters; it requires a Lipschitz critic, enforced by weight clipping or a gradient penalty.

Q: In DDPM-style diffusion, the forward process [E]
- removes noise step by step
* gradually adds Gaussian noise to data over many steps according to a fixed schedule
- is learned by a neural network
- maps noise to data in one step
> The forward (noising) process is fixed; the network learns the reverse process that denoises.

Q: A convenient property of the Gaussian forward process is that x_t can be sampled directly from x_0 as [M]
- x_t = x_0 + t
* x_t = sqrt(alpha_bar_t) x_0 + sqrt(1 - alpha_bar_t) eps
- x_t = alpha_t x_(t-1) only by iterating all steps
- x_t = eps / x_0
> Because Gaussians compose, any timestep can be reached in closed form, which makes training on random timesteps efficient.

Q: The standard "simple" training loss for DDPM is [M]
- binary cross-entropy between real and fake
* mean squared error between the true noise and the network's predicted noise
- KL divergence between the image and the prior
- perceptual loss with a VGG network
> The network eps_theta(x_t, t) regresses the noise that was added; this is a reweighted variational bound.

Q: Classifier-free guidance generates samples using [M]
- a separately trained classifier's gradient
* eps = eps_uncond + w * (eps_cond - eps_uncond), from a model trained with the condition randomly dropped
- two different diffusion models with different schedules
- rejection sampling
> One network learns conditional and unconditional predictions; extrapolating toward the conditional one trades diversity for fidelity and prompt adherence.

Q: Latent diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion run the diffusion process [M]
- directly on pixels at full resolution
* in the compressed latent space of a pretrained autoencoder
- on text tokens
- in the frequency domain of the image only
> Diffusing in a lower-dimensional latent (for example 8x down-sampled) cuts computation greatly; the decoder maps latents back to pixels.

Q: DDIM sampling differs from DDPM ancestral sampling in that it [H]
- requires retraining the model
* uses a deterministic (or less stochastic) non-Markovian update that allows far fewer steps with the same trained network
- adds more noise at every step
- works only for audio
> DDIM defines a family of processes sharing the same training objective; the deterministic variant corresponds to solving the probability-flow ODE.

Q: Flow matching trains a network to [H]
- classify noise levels
* regress a velocity field that transports samples from a simple prior to the data along prescribed probability paths
- maximize a variational bound with a discriminator
- predict the next token
> With straight-line (rectified flow) paths x_t = (1 - t) x_0 + t x_1 the target velocity is x_1 - x_0; sampling integrates an ODE and needs few steps.

Q: Normalizing flows compute exact likelihoods because [M]
- they use a discriminator
* they are invertible mappings with tractable Jacobian determinants, so the change-of-variables formula applies
- they discretize the data
- they ignore the latent space
> log p(x) = log p(z) + log |det dz/dx|. Architectures such as coupling layers make the determinant cheap.

Q: Autoregressive generative models factorize the joint distribution as [E]
- a product of independent marginals
* a product of conditionals, each variable given the previous ones
- a sum of Gaussians
- a single softmax over all possible outputs
> p(x) = prod_i p(x_i | x_<i) by the chain rule; this gives exact likelihoods but sequential sampling.

Q: Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) compares generated and real images by [M]
- pixel-wise mean squared error
* the distance between Gaussians fitted to their Inception-network feature distributions
- the discriminator's accuracy
- counting distinct images
> Lower FID is better; it captures both quality and diversity but depends on sample size and the feature extractor.

Q: Compared with GANs, diffusion models generally offer [M]
- faster sampling
* more stable training and better mode coverage, at the cost of slower iterative sampling
- no need for a neural network
- exact likelihoods for free in all cases
> Diffusion optimizes a simple regression loss without adversarial dynamics; distillation and consistency models reduce the number of sampling steps.

Q: The beta-VAE increases the weight on the KL term in order to [M]
- improve reconstruction sharpness
* encourage more disentangled latent factors, at some cost in reconstruction quality
- remove the prior
- speed up training
> A larger beta pressures the posterior toward the factorized prior, favouring independent latent dimensions.

Q: VQ-VAE differs from a standard VAE in that [M]
- it has no decoder
* its latent space is discrete: encoder outputs are snapped to the nearest vector in a learned codebook
- it uses a Gaussian mixture decoder
- it cannot be combined with autoregressive priors
> Quantization uses a straight-through gradient; a prior over code indices (autoregressive or diffusion) is trained afterwards. This underlies many image and audio tokenizers.

Q: Predicting "v" (velocity) or x_0 instead of noise in diffusion training is mainly a choice of [H]
- network architecture
* parameterization, which changes the loss weighting across noise levels and can improve stability
- dataset
- optimizer
> The three targets are linear functions of each other given x_t; they differ in how errors at high and low noise are weighted.

Q: Consistency models aim to [H]
- remove the need for training data
* map any point on a diffusion trajectory directly to its clean endpoint, enabling one- or few-step generation
- increase the number of sampling steps
- learn a discriminator for diffusion
> They are trained (by distillation or from scratch) so that outputs agree along a trajectory, collapsing the iterative solver.

Q: In score-based generative modeling, the "score" is [H]
- the classifier's confidence
* the gradient of the log data density with respect to the data
- the FID of the model
- the discriminator's output
> Denoising score matching learns grad_x log p_t(x); predicting the noise in DDPM is equivalent up to scaling.

Q: The generator of a conditional GAN or a diffusion model is conditioned on a class label most simply by [E]
- changing the optimizer
* feeding a learned embedding of the label into the network together with the noise or timestep
- training one model per pixel
- removing normalization
> Label embeddings are added or concatenated, or modulate normalization layers; guidance then strengthens the conditioning.

Q: Diffusion Transformers (DiT) replace the U-Net backbone with [M]
- an LSTM
* a Transformer operating on patches of the (latent) image
- a decision tree
- a normalizing flow
> Patchified latents are processed as tokens with timestep and condition injected through adaptive normalization; scaling the Transformer improves sample quality.

Q: The cosine noise schedule was proposed for diffusion because the original linear schedule [H]
- was too slow to compute
* destroys information too quickly, leaving many late timesteps as nearly pure noise, especially at low resolution
- added no noise
- required labels
> A cosine alpha_bar decays more gradually in the middle of the process, making more timesteps informative.
