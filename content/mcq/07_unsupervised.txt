Q: k-means minimizes [E]
- the distance between cluster centroids
* the sum of squared distances from each point to its assigned centroid
- the number of clusters
- the negative log-likelihood of a mixture with full covariances
> The objective is the within-cluster sum of squares (inertia); the assignment and update steps each never increase it.

Q: Lloyd's algorithm for k-means is guaranteed to [M]
- find the global optimum
* converge to a local optimum in a finite number of steps
- produce clusters of equal size
- be independent of initialization
> Each step decreases the objective and there are finitely many partitions, so it converges, but only to a local minimum that depends on initialization.

Q: k-means++ improves on random initialization by [M]
- running k-means several times
* choosing initial centroids far apart, sampling each with probability proportional to squared distance from the nearest chosen centroid
- using medians instead of means
- choosing k automatically
> Spreading out the initial centres gives an O(log k) approximation guarantee in expectation and usually faster convergence.

Q: Which clustering method can find clusters of arbitrary shape and labels low-density points as noise? [E]
- k-means
- Gaussian mixture with spherical covariance
* DBSCAN
- PCA
> DBSCAN grows clusters from dense regions using eps and min_samples, does not require k, and marks sparse points as outliers.

Q: In the EM algorithm for a Gaussian mixture, the E-step computes [M]
- new means and covariances
* the posterior responsibilities of each component for each point
- the number of components
- the gradient of the log-likelihood
> The E-step evaluates p(z = k | x_i) under current parameters; the M-step re-estimates weights, means and covariances using those soft assignments.

Q: k-means can be seen as a limiting case of a Gaussian mixture model with [M]
- full covariance matrices and soft assignments
* equal spherical covariances whose variance goes to zero, giving hard assignments
- diagonal covariances and unequal mixing weights
- a single component
> As the shared isotropic variance shrinks, responsibilities become one-hot and EM reduces to Lloyd's algorithm.

Q: Each iteration of EM is guaranteed to [M]
- reach the global maximum of the likelihood
* not decrease the data log-likelihood
- decrease the number of components
- increase the entropy of the responsibilities
> EM maximizes a lower bound that touches the log-likelihood at the current parameters, so the likelihood is monotonically non-decreasing.

Q: The first principal component is the direction that [E]
- minimizes the variance of the projected data
* maximizes the variance of the projected data
- separates the classes best
- is parallel to the first feature axis
> PCA finds orthogonal directions of maximal variance, equivalently minimizing squared reconstruction error.

Q: The fraction of variance explained by the first k principal components is [E]
- k divided by the number of features
* the sum of the top k eigenvalues of the covariance matrix divided by the sum of all eigenvalues
- the largest eigenvalue
- the determinant of the covariance matrix
> Each eigenvalue is the variance along its component, and the total variance is the trace of the covariance matrix.

Q: Before applying PCA to features with very different units, you should usually [E]
- shuffle the features
* standardize the features
- one-hot encode them
- take their absolute values
> PCA is variance-based, so a feature with a large numeric scale would dominate the components unless the data are standardized.

Q: A linear autoencoder with a k-unit bottleneck trained with squared error learns [M]
- a random projection
* the same subspace as the top k principal components
- a non-linear manifold
- the identity function exactly
> The optimal linear encoder/decoder spans the principal subspace, though the individual directions need not be orthogonal or ordered.

Q: t-SNE visualizations should be read with caution because [M]
- t-SNE is a linear projection
* distances between clusters and cluster sizes in the embedding are not reliable; mainly local neighbourhoods are preserved
- it always produces the same output
- it needs labels
> t-SNE matches neighbour distributions with a KL objective; global geometry and densities are distorted and results depend on perplexity and seed.

Q: The silhouette score of a point compares [M]
- its distance to the origin with the cluster radius
* its mean distance to points in its own cluster with its mean distance to points in the nearest other cluster
- its label with the predicted cluster
- the within-cluster variance before and after clustering
> s = (b - a) / max(a, b), ranging from -1 to 1; values near 1 indicate well-separated, cohesive clusters.

Q: The "elbow method" for choosing k in k-means looks for [E]
- the k with zero inertia
* the k after which inertia decreases much more slowly
- the k with the highest inertia
- the k equal to the number of features
> Inertia always decreases with k; the elbow marks diminishing returns. It is a heuristic and often ambiguous.

Q: Agglomerative hierarchical clustering with single linkage merges the two clusters with [M]
- the smallest distance between their centroids
* the smallest distance between their closest pair of points
- the smallest distance between their farthest pair of points
- the smallest increase in variance
> Single linkage uses the minimum pairwise distance and tends to produce chained clusters; complete linkage uses the maximum, Ward's the variance increase.

Q: Which is an advantage of a Gaussian mixture model over k-means? [M]
- It always converges faster
* It gives soft assignments and can model elliptical clusters of different sizes
- It has no hyper-parameters
- It is immune to local optima
> GMMs estimate covariances and mixing weights, so clusters need not be spherical or equally sized, and each point gets membership probabilities.

Q: BIC and AIC are used with mixture models to [M]
- initialize the means
* select the number of components by penalizing model complexity
- compute responsibilities
- speed up the M-step
> Likelihood always improves with more components; information criteria add a penalty on the number of parameters to choose a parsimonious model.

Q: An autoencoder is used for anomaly detection by flagging inputs with [E]
- a small latent norm
* a high reconstruction error
- a low reconstruction error
- a large batch size
> Trained on normal data, the autoencoder reconstructs typical inputs well; unusual inputs are reconstructed poorly.

Q: Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) differs from PCA in that [M]
- it requires labels
* its factors are constrained to be non-negative, giving additive, parts-based representations
- it produces orthogonal components
- it has a closed-form solution via SVD
> Non-negativity prevents cancellation between components, which often yields interpretable parts (topics, facial parts) at the cost of a non-convex problem.

Q: Kernel PCA extends PCA by [H]
- adding class labels
* performing PCA in an implicit feature space defined by a kernel, via the eigendecomposition of the centred Gram matrix
- using an L1 penalty on the loadings
- replacing variance with entropy
> Working with the n x n kernel matrix captures non-linear structure without computing the feature map explicitly.

Q: k-means struggles most with which kind of data? [M]
- Well-separated spherical clusters of similar size
* Elongated or non-convex clusters with very different densities
- Low-dimensional data
- Data with exactly k distinct points
> Assigning to the nearest centroid assumes roughly isotropic clusters of similar scale; rings, crescents and uneven densities violate this.

Q: The time complexity of one iteration of Lloyd's k-means on n points in d dimensions with k clusters is [M]
- O(n)
* O(n k d)
- O(n^2 d)
- O(k^n)
> Each point's distance to each centroid is computed, costing O(n k d); the centroid update is O(n d).

Q: UMAP, compared with t-SNE, is generally [M]
- slower and limited to two dimensions
* faster, better at preserving some global structure, and usable for general dimensionality reduction
- a linear technique
- deterministic regardless of seed
> UMAP builds a fuzzy neighbour graph and optimizes a cross-entropy between graphs; it scales well and supports embedding into arbitrary dimensions and transforming new points.

Q: Whitening a dataset means transforming it so that [M]
- all values lie in [0, 1]
* the features are uncorrelated and each has unit variance
- every sample has unit norm
- the mean of each sample is zero
> Whitening rotates the data to its principal axes and rescales each by 1/sqrt(eigenvalue), giving an identity covariance matrix.

Q: Spectral clustering clusters the data by [H]
- thresholding the largest singular value
* running k-means on the leading eigenvectors of a graph Laplacian built from pairwise similarities
- fitting Gaussians in the frequency domain
- hierarchically merging the closest points
> The eigenvectors of the Laplacian embed the similarity graph so that weakly connected groups separate, which handles non-convex clusters.
