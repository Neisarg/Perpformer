# topic: Linear & Logistic Regression
# id: linear
# subtopics: Ordinary least squares and its assumptions; Ridge, lasso and elastic net; Logistic regression and the sigmoid; Softmax regression; Generalized linear models; Multicollinearity and feature scaling; Interpreting coefficients

Q: Minimizing mean squared error in linear regression is equivalent to maximum likelihood under the assumption that the noise is [E]
- Laplace distributed
* Gaussian with constant variance
- uniformly distributed
- Poisson distributed
> The Gaussian log-likelihood is, up to constants, the negative sum of squared residuals. Laplace noise corresponds to mean absolute error.

Q: Ridge regression changes the OLS solution to [E]
- (X^T X)^-1 X^T y + lambda
* (X^T X + lambda I)^-1 X^T y
- (X^T X - lambda I)^-1 X^T y
- lambda (X^T X)^-1 X^T y
> Adding lambda ||w||^2 to the objective adds lambda I to X^T X, which also makes the system well-conditioned and always invertible.

Q: Which regularizer performs automatic feature selection by driving some coefficients exactly to zero? [E]
- Ridge (L2)
* Lasso (L1)
- Dropout
- Early stopping
> The corners of the L1 ball lie on the axes, so the optimum often has exactly zero coefficients.

Q: With two perfectly correlated features, lasso tends to [M]
- assign both the same coefficient
* pick one of them somewhat arbitrarily and zero the other
- fail to converge
- give both an infinite coefficient
> Lasso has no unique solution among duplicated features and typically keeps one. Ridge splits the weight evenly; elastic net combines both behaviours.

Q: Logistic regression models [E]
- the class label as a linear function of the features
* the log-odds of the positive class as a linear function of the features
- the probability as a linear function of the features
- the features as a Gaussian given the class
> log(p / (1 - p)) = w^T x + b. The probability is the sigmoid of the linear score.

Q: The gradient of the binary cross-entropy loss with respect to the logit z, for prediction p = sigmoid(z) and label y, is [M]
- y - p
* p - y
- p (1 - p)
- (p - y) p (1 - p)
> The sigmoid derivative cancels with the cross-entropy derivative, leaving p - y. This is why cross-entropy avoids the saturation problem of MSE on sigmoid outputs.

Q: Logistic regression trained on linearly separable data without regularization will [M]
- converge to a unique finite solution
* have weights that grow without bound as the likelihood keeps increasing
- fail on the first iteration
- learn a non-linear decision boundary
> Scaling a separating weight vector increases the likelihood, so the norm diverges; regularization or early stopping gives a finite solution.

Q: In logistic regression, a coefficient of 0.7 on feature x_j means that a one-unit increase in x_j [M]
- increases the predicted probability by 0.7
- multiplies the predicted probability by 0.7
* multiplies the odds of the positive class by e^0.7, about 2
- increases the odds by 0.7
> Coefficients act additively on log-odds, hence multiplicatively on the odds; exp(0.7) is about 2.01.

Q: Multicollinearity among features in OLS primarily causes [M]
- biased predictions
* unstable coefficient estimates with large variance
- a non-convex loss
- heteroscedastic residuals
> Predictions can remain fine, but X^T X becomes nearly singular, so individual coefficients are poorly determined and sensitive to small data changes.

Q: R-squared on the training set, when you add an additional feature to an OLS model, [E]
- always decreases
* never decreases
- stays exactly the same
- can go either way
> The larger model can always reproduce the smaller one by setting the new coefficient to zero, so training fit cannot get worse. Adjusted R-squared penalizes this.

Q: Feature standardization matters most for which of the following? [M]
- OLS predictions solved in closed form
- Decision trees
* Ridge or lasso regression
- Naive Bayes with categorical features
> Penalties on coefficient size depend on feature scale, so unscaled features are penalized unequally. Unregularized OLS predictions and trees are scale-invariant.

Q: Softmax regression with K classes and d features, with a bias per class, has how many parameters? [E]
- d + K
* K (d + 1)
- d^K
- K^2 d
> One weight vector of size d plus one bias for each of the K classes. (One class's parameters are redundant, but the standard parameterization keeps all K.)

Q: Which loss is most robust to outliers in the target variable? [M]
- Mean squared error
* Huber loss
- Squared hinge loss
- Exponential loss
> Huber is quadratic near zero and linear for large residuals, limiting the influence of outliers that would dominate squared error.

Q: Poisson regression, a generalized linear model for count data, uses which link function by default? [M]
- Identity
- Logit
* Log
- Probit
> The canonical link for the Poisson is the log: log E[y] = w^T x, which keeps the predicted mean positive.

Q: Heteroscedasticity in a linear regression means [M]
- the features are correlated with each other
* the variance of the errors is not constant across observations
- the errors are not centred at zero
- the relationship is non-linear
> With non-constant error variance OLS coefficients stay unbiased but are no longer efficient, and the usual standard errors are wrong.

Q: Why is MSE a poor loss for training a classifier with a sigmoid output? [M]
- It is not differentiable
* The gradient contains a factor p(1-p) that vanishes when the unit saturates, even if the prediction is badly wrong
- It cannot be minimized by gradient descent
- It always gives a convex objective, which overfits
> With MSE the gradient with respect to the logit is (p - y) p (1 - p); confident wrong predictions get tiny gradients. The loss is also non-convex in the weights.

Q: Elastic net combines [E]
- ridge with dropout
* L1 and L2 penalties
- lasso with early stopping
- OLS with PCA
> Elastic net adds both penalties, keeping lasso's sparsity while handling groups of correlated features more stably.

Q: The decision boundary of binary logistic regression in feature space is [E]
- a sigmoid curve
* a hyperplane
- a circle
- piecewise constant
> p = 0.5 exactly where w^T x + b = 0, which is a hyperplane. Non-linear boundaries require feature transformations.

Q: Leverage and Cook's distance are diagnostics for [H]
- multicollinearity
* influential observations in regression
- non-normality of features
- autocorrelation of residuals
> High-leverage points have unusual feature values; Cook's distance measures how much the fit changes when a point is removed. Multicollinearity is diagnosed with VIF.

Q: Using one-hot encoding for a categorical feature with K levels together with an intercept in OLS, you should drop one level because otherwise [M]
- the model becomes non-linear
* the design matrix is rank deficient (the dummy variable trap)
- predictions become biased
- the loss is no longer convex
> The K dummies sum to the intercept column, making X^T X singular. Dropping one level or regularizing removes the redundancy.

Q: Which optimization method is classically used to fit logistic regression and is equivalent to Newton's method? [H]
- Coordinate ascent on the dual
* Iteratively reweighted least squares
- Expectation maximization
- Simulated annealing
> Newton's update for the logistic likelihood can be written as a weighted least squares problem with weights p(1-p), solved repeatedly (IRLS).

Q: A linear regression has high training error and similar high validation error. The most appropriate next step is [E]
- adding more L2 regularization
- collecting more training rows of the same kind
* adding features or non-linear terms to increase model capacity
- reducing the number of features
> Similar high errors on both sets indicate underfitting (high bias), which is addressed by a more expressive model or better features.

Q: With class-imbalanced data, down-sampling the negative class by a factor r before fitting logistic regression mainly distorts [H]
- the coefficient of every feature equally
* the intercept: predicted log-odds are shifted by a constant that can be corrected afterwards
- nothing, the model is unchanged
- the convexity of the loss
> Sampling on the label changes the prior odds, which appears as a shift of log(r) in the intercept; the feature coefficients are asymptotically unaffected. Subtract log(r) from the logit to recover calibrated probabilities.

Q: The bias term in a linear model [E]
- should be heavily regularized like other weights
* lets the fitted hyperplane not pass through the origin and is usually left unregularized
- is equivalent to feature scaling
- makes the model non-linear
> The intercept captures the baseline level of the target. Penalizing it would make the solution depend on an arbitrary shift of y.

Q: In the probabilistic view, the sigmoid arises naturally in binary classification when [H]
- the class-conditional densities are arbitrary
* the class-conditional densities are Gaussians with a shared covariance
- the features are discrete
- the prior over classes is uniform
> For exponential-family class conditionals with shared dispersion (such as shared-covariance Gaussians, i.e. LDA), the posterior log-odds are linear in x, giving a sigmoid of a linear function.
